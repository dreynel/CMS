INTRODUCTION
[1] (Heading 2) Background of the Study
[2] (Body Text Indented) Computers and software are very helpful in running modern stores and coffee shops. In a cafe, a Point of Sale or POS system helps staff take orders quickly, compute prices and discounts correctly, and print customer receipts without making math mistakes. Using Quick Response (QR) codes makes counter work even faster because products and customer passes are identified right away. At the same time, an online booking website helps customers see available seats ahead of time, which prevents long lines and crowded dining areas.
[3] (Body Text Indented) At Iloilo Science and Technology University - Dumangas Campus (ISAT U - DC) in Dumangas, Iloilo, there is an existing Mini Hotel. The school uses this hotel for campus guests, seminars, and student training. The university is now planning to open a new coffee shop and study lounge inside this mini hotel. This new coffee shop will sell coffee, cold drinks, and snacks to students, teachers, and visitors. It will also have tables and quiet study pods with power outlets where students can do their homework and charge their laptops.
[4] (Body Text Indented) To run the upcoming coffee shop efficiently, the operations are divided between two separate staff members: one person works as the cashier at the front counter, and another person works as the barista who prepares the beverages at the kitchen station. However, relying on manual pen-and-paper communication between the cashier and the barista creates serious operational bottlenecks. When the cashier writes orders on loose paper chits and carries or calls them out to the barista, slips can easily get wet, torn, stained by spilled milk, or lost on the prep table. During peak class breaks, verbal shouting and messy handwriting lead to order mix-ups, wrong cup sizes, forgotten modifiers, and delayed service.
[5] (Body Text Indented) To prevent these problems before the coffee shop opens, the researchers created a computerized management system. Setting up a computerized system before opening makes sure the new coffee shop starts with a smooth, paperless, and organized way of working from day one.
[6] (Body Text Indented) The researchers developed the ISAT U - DC Coffee Shop POS System with Online Reservation and QR Code. The system provides dedicated interfaces for each separate user. Customers can visit the website to view seat layouts, reserve study pods with power outlets, select pre-ordered beverages and snacks, agree to the mandatory 15-minute seating reservation policy, and manage their booking through a dynamic QR booking pass. At the service counter, the staff concierge verifies guest arrival and seating assignments through QR scanning, while the cashier uses the touch POS terminal to scan the customer's QR pass—instantly auto-fetching the pre-ordered items into the sales cart for review, optional editing, and payment processing—as well as scanning printed product QR code tags for walk-in items, verifying packaging availability, applying statutory discounts, and issuing thermal sales receipts. At the beverage preparation area, the barista uses a digital Kitchen Display System (KDS) screen to view confirmed POS orders alongside each item's exact base recipe ingredients, selected add-ons, and required cups, straws, and takeout boxes—atomically deducting raw ingredients and packaging from the perpetual inventory ledger upon brewing and serving. The system also gives administrators a perpetual inventory suite with datalist-validated Stock In/Out vouchers, item conversions, recipe and add-on configuration, printable menu QR code tag sheets, and sales audit dashboards.
[7] (Body Text Indented) By using this system, the upcoming coffee shop inside the ISAT U - DC Mini Hotel will have an easy and organized way to serve customers, coordinate orders between the cashier and barista seamlessly, manage study pods, and track sales, recipes, packaging, and raw ingredient inventory without using loose paper slips.

[14] (Objective Item) 4. Develop a POS module that supports product QR code scanning, order processing, payment recording, discount computation, and receipt generation.
[15] (Objective Item) 5. Develop a Kitchen Display System (KDS) for displaying and updating beverage and food order statuses.
[16] (Objective Item) 6. Implement inventory monitoring and sales reporting functions.
[17] (Objective Item) 7. Evaluate the system using the applicable ISO/IEC 25010 software product quality characteristics.

[25] (Heading 2) Scope and Limitations
[26] (Heading 3) Scope
[27] (Body Text Indented) The scope of the study focuses on the design, development, and evaluation of an integrated web-based Point of Sale (POS) and online reservation system engineered specifically for the upcoming coffee shop and study lounge situated inside the ISAT U - Dumangas Campus Mini Hotel in Dumangas, Iloilo. The software encompasses four interconnected functional modules tailored to distinct user roles: an online reservation and pre-ordering portal for customers, a touch-enabled front-counter POS terminal for the cashier, a dark-mode Kitchen Display System (KDS) for the barista, and an administrative control panel for campus supervisors.
[28] (Body Text Indented) The customer portal enables students, faculty, and mini hotel guests to inspect real-time seating availability across designated zones (Lounge, Study Hub, Main Hall, and Terrace), book a study pod or dining table, pre-order customized beverages with specific sweetness and ice modifiers, and receive an encrypted dynamic QR pass for seamless check-in. The front-counter cashier module features optical 2D QR code auto-reading for rapid product classification, cup size and modifier adjustments, statutory discount computation (comprising a 10% discount for ISAT U students and faculty members and a 20% statutory discount for senior citizens and persons with disabilities [PWD]), cash tender and GCash payment recording, thermal receipt generation, and rapid customer QR pass validation. The kitchen operations module provides a dedicated real-time KDS screen with acoustic chimes for beverage and food preparation tracking, and triggers Brew-to-Serve inventory deductions. The administrative module provides user account management, product pricing and recipe configuration, printable menu QR code generation, perpetual inventory tracking, and daily sales auditing.
[29] (Heading 3) Limitations
[30] (Body Text Indented) The operational scope of the ISAT U - DC Coffee Shop POS System is subject to several practical limitations. First, while the system provides structured recording of digital payments, it does not interface directly with commercial credit card banking gateways; cashless payments conducted via digital wallets such as GCash require manual verification of reference numbers by the counter cashier. Additionally, the system operates as a responsive web-based platform accessible through standard mobile and desktop browsers rather than a native downloadable mobile application, and all booking passes and transactional notifications are delivered digitally through the web portal without automated cellular SMS transmission.
[31] (Body Text Indented) Furthermore, the software is engineered exclusively for on-campus dining, study pod reservations, and counter takeout within the coffee shop and study lounge situated inside the ISAT U - Dumangas Campus Mini Hotel, and does not integrate with external third-party food delivery services or broader multi-branch hotel management infrastructures. Physical access to study pods and reserved tables relies on visual check-in and staff concierge verification rather than automated electronic door latch mechanisms. Finally, system availability depends upon the active campus local area network and continuous institutional electrical power; severe network interruptions or power outages impair real-time portal access until network connectivity is restored.

[39] (Heading 2) Definition of Terms
[40] (Body Text Indented) For a better understanding of the terms used in this study, the following technical and operational terms are conceptually and operationally defined:
[41] (Body Text Indented) Barista. A skilled beverage preparation specialist responsible for crafting coffee, teas, and specialty drinks. A system user role operating the Kitchen Display System (KDS) interface to view live incoming orders, prepare items according to exact recipe bills of materials (BOM), update ticket statuses, and trigger inventory deduction upon order fulfillment.
[42] (Body Text Indented) Brew-to-Serve Inventory Deduction. A perpetual inventory control technique where raw materials and packaging supplies are deducted from stock only when finished products are physically produced and dispensed. An automated system routine executed in the ISAT U system when the barista advances an order from Brewing to Ready/Served, decrementing exact recipe ingredient quantities, cups, straws, and takeout boxes in the database without stock trapping at the POS checkout counter.
[43] (Body Text Indented) Cashier. A frontline retail worker responsible for recording customer orders, processing financial transactions, and issuing proof of payment. A system user role accessing the touch POS terminal to scan product QR codes, verify reservation QR passes, compute statutory discounts and taxes, log cash tender or GCash reference numbers, and print thermal receipts.
[44] (Body Text Indented) Dynamic QR Pass. A time-sensitive, machine-readable two-dimensional matrix barcode containing encoded transactional or authorization tokens. A cryptographically unique QR code generated by the web reservation portal upon booking confirmation, presented by the customer on a mobile device and scanned by the staff concierge for seating verification and by the cashier to auto-fetch pre-selected items into the POS sales cart for review, payment, and receipt generation.
[45] (Body Text Indented) Kitchen Display System (KDS). A digital kitchen communication monitor that visualizes active culinary orders in real time, replacing physical handwritten chits. A dedicated web-based touch screen interface that renders chronological order cards with countdown timers, audio alert chimes, recipe add-ons, and interactive status buttons for the barista.
[46] (Body Text Indented) Online Reservation Portal. A web-based booking system allowing remote users to schedule resource allocation and reserve facilities in advance. The customer-facing web application of the ISAT U coffee shop where users inspect real-time seating availability across four zones, book study pods or dining tables, and select beverage pre-orders.
[47] (Body Text Indented) Point of Sale (POS) Transaction and Terminal. The computerized hardware and software environment where a commercial sales transaction is completed, item prices are aggregated, and payments are settled. The dedicated counter terminal interface used by the cashier to process walk-in and pre-order transactions, apply 10% student/faculty and 20% senior/PWD discounts, calculate 12% VAT, and record revenue in the MySQL database.
[48] (Body Text Indented) QR Code Product Classification and Scanning. The automated optical identification of retail items using standard 2D matrix symbology (ISO/IEC 18004) to retrieve product attributes without manual typing. The process of tagging coffee shop menu items with unique QR codes, which are decoded by the cashier's optical camera or 2D scanner to automatically populate the POS sales cart with product title, base price, and recipe modifiers.
[49] (Body Text Indented) Study Pod. A semi-private, acoustically buffered individual workspace furnished with electrical outlets and seating for focused academic activity. A designated bookable seating unit within the Study Hub and Lounge zones of the ISAT U Mini Hotel, tracked in the database by unique identifiers, electrical availability, and reservation time slots.

[53] (Heading 2) Related Literature
[54] (Heading 3) ACID Transactional Integrity and Point of Sale Architecture
[55] (Body Text Indented) Coronel and Morris (2020), in Database Systems: Design, Implementation, & Management (13th ed., Cengage Learning), together with Laudon and Laudon (2022) in Management Information Systems: Managing the Digital Firm (17th ed., Pearson), underscore that enterprise retail and Point of Sale systems fundamentally depend upon the ACID model (Atomicity, Consistency, Isolation, and Durability) to maintain financial and inventory integrity. In multi-user hospitality environments where counter sales, seat bookings, and kitchen orders occur concurrently, atomic transactions guarantee that database operations—such as recording payments, issuing thermal receipts, and executing stock ledger updates—succeed completely or roll back entirely, thereby preventing orphaned records or cash-inventory discrepancies. The ISAT U system adopts this architectural principle by wrapping POS order logging, table occupancy changes, and Brew-to-Serve ingredient deductions in explicit PDO database transactions.
[56] (Body Text Indented) Coronel & Morris, 2020; Laudon & Laudon, 2022
[57] (Heading 3) Role-Based Access Control in Multi-User Service Platforms
[58] (Body Text Indented) Ferraiolo, Kuhn, and Chandramouli (2020), in Role-Based Access Control (2nd ed., Artech House), emphasize that enterprise information systems serving multiple organizational tiers require strict Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) to enforce the principle of least privilege. Segregating privileges among administrative personnel, frontline service staff, kitchen operators, and external patrons safeguards sensitive business metrics, prevents unauthorized pricing alterations, and provides an auditable operational trail. In the ISAT U coffee shop system, RBAC is strictly enforced via server-side session middleware, ensuring that customers access only public seating and ordering views, cashiers operate restricted sales registers, baristas interact exclusively with kitchen queue controllers, and administrators manage menu pricing, printable QR tags, and inventory parameters.
[59] (Body Text Indented) Ferraiolo, Kuhn, & Chandramouli, 2020
[60] (Heading 3) Two-Dimensional QR Matrix Symbology in Retail and Resource Management
[61] (Body Text Indented) The International Organization for Standardization and International Electrotechnical Commission (ISO/IEC 18004:2015), along with empirical analysis by Soon (2008), demonstrate that Quick Response (QR) matrix symbology provides significantly superior data density, error correction capability (up to 30% damage tolerance via Reed-Solomon coding), and 360-degree high-speed readability compared to legacy one-dimensional barcodes. These characteristics make QR codes uniquely suited for both mobile customer ticketing and optical retail classification. The ISAT U system leverages ISO/IEC 18004 standards in a dual capacity: generating printable 2D QR tags for instant product classification at the cashier counter, and generating dynamic, encrypted QR passes on customer smartphones for rapid check-in and pre-order retrieval.
[62] (Body Text Indented) ISO/IEC 18004:2015; Soon, 2008
[63] (Heading 3) Kitchen Display Systems and Real-Time Service Queue Coordination
[64] (Body Text Indented) Davis and Stone (2020), writing in the International Journal of Hospitality Management, demonstrated that transitioning from traditional physical paper chits to digital Kitchen Display Systems (KDS) in commercial food and beverage operations dramatically mitigates order transcription errors, eliminates lost tickets, and reduces ticket fulfillment latency by providing real-time acoustic alerts, color-coded urgency queues, and exact recipe modifier displays. Furthermore, modern KDS architectures allow decoupling POS payment from stock consumption, enabling perpetual inventory deduction only when items are physically prepared and served. The ISAT U system implements this pattern through its dark-mode Barista KDS, which presents incoming order queues with sound chimes, recipe bills of materials, and automated Brew-to-Serve inventory deductions upon drink preparation.

[90] (Heading 2) Synthesis of the Study
[91] (Body Text Indented) A rigorous analysis of the reviewed literature and empirical studies reveals a distinct research and operational gap in existing campus commercial platforms. Previous investigations have typically addressed commercial point of sale, facility scheduling, and kitchen management in isolation. Traditional campus retail systems either focus purely on front-counter sales without reservation capabilities, manage room bookings without food and beverage ordering, or lack digital kitchen display coordination. Furthermore, conventional retail POS software enforces counter-level stock trapping, erroneously assuming that finished drinks are pre-manufactured stock units rather than made-to-order artisanal beverages requiring recipe-based preparation. Prior to this research, no documented platform integrated space reservation for quiet study pods with power outlets, optical 2D QR product classification, paperless kitchen queue dispatching, and perpetual recipe-based stock control within a unified campus mini hotel environment.
[92] (Body Text Indented) Methodologically and architecturally, the proposed ISAT U - DC Coffee Shop POS System diverges significantly from previous works. While earlier studies often relied on rigid waterfall models or generic third-party commercial software, this study employs the Prototyping Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) Model. This iterative methodology enabled continuous design refinement based on hands-on evaluations by university administrators, auxiliary staff, and prospective student patrons. Architecturally, the system decouples sales recording from inventory depletion through an automated 'Brew-to-Serve' mechanism. Frontline cashiers record orders, compute statutory discounts (10% student/faculty and 20% senior/PWD), and issue thermal receipts without altering ingredient balances; raw materials, packaging cups, straws, and takeout boxes are deducted only when the barista physically prepares and serves the order on the KDS screen. Additionally, the system leverages ISO/IEC 18004 2D QR matrix symbology in a unified dual capacity—generating printable menu QR tags for instantaneous cashier cart auto-reading, and issuing encrypted, dynamic QR passes on customer smartphones for rapid concierge check-in and scheduled pre-order dispatch.
[93] (Body Text Indented) The primary contribution of this study is the delivery of a customized, cost-effective, and empirically validated operational management prototype developed from the ground up for the upcoming coffee shop inside the ISAT U - Dumangas Campus Mini Hotel. By synthesizing online facility booking, optical QR item scanning, acoustic kitchen signaling, and perpetual ingredient tracking into a responsive Native PHP and MySQL architecture, the system provides a dependable institutional blueprint for university auxiliary enterprises, demonstrating that modern web technologies and 2D matrix symbology can elevate operational efficiency, prevent inventory wastage, and enhance the academic hospitality experience.

METHODOLOGY
[96] (Heading 2) Project Description
[97] (Body Text Indented) The ISAT U - DC Coffee Shop POS System with Online Reservation and QR Code is an integrated, web-based management platform engineered from the ground up for the upcoming coffee shop and study lounge situated inside the ISAT U - Dumangas Campus Mini Hotel in Dumangas, Iloilo. The system serves as a unified operational bridge connecting front-of-house customer transactions, counter cashiering, kitchen beverage brewing, and back-office administrative oversight.
[98] (Body Text Indented) Through its responsive architecture, customers can access self-service seating reservations across four distinct hotel zones (Lounge, Study Hub, Main Hall, and Terrace), pre-order customized beverages with specific sweetness and ice modifiers, and obtain an encrypted dynamic QR pass for seamless check-in. At the service counter, the cashier operates a touch-enabled POS terminal paired with an optical 2D scanner to read product QR tags for instant classification, apply statutory student, faculty, senior, and PWD discounts, record cash or GCash payments, and print thermal sales receipts. In the beverage preparation area, the barista utilizes a paperless Kitchen Display System (KDS) monitor with audio alert chimes to inspect recipe bills of materials and advance tickets through preparation stages, automatically triggering Brew-to-Serve inventory deductions upon drink fulfillment. Meanwhile, campus administrators utilize centralized dashboards to configure user accounts, adjust menu prices, generate printable product QR tags, monitor perpetual ingredient balances, and audit daily sales revenue.

[100] (Body Text Indented) To develop the system from the ground up for the upcoming campus enterprise, the researchers adopted the Prototyping Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) Model. The Prototyping Model is an iterative software engineering methodology that constructs early functional versions of the system, subjects them to direct stakeholder evaluation, and refines system features through continuous feedback cycles until all operational specifications are fulfilled. This model is exceptionally well-suited for novel campus auxiliary enterprises where end-user requirements and physical workflow configurations evolve through direct operational testing. [109] (Heading 3) Phase 4: User Evaluation
[110] (Body Text Indented) The prototype was demonstrated to prospective coffee shop cashiers, baristas, campus supervisors, and student evaluators at ISAT U - DC. [111] (Heading 3) Phase 5: Prototype Refinement
[112] (Body Text Indented) Based on evaluator feedback, the researchers executed iterative refinements: optimizing camera auto-focus for faster QR decoding, integrating audible alert chimes on the barista KDS screen, adding dedicated 10% student/faculty and 20% senior/PWD discount buttons, implementing POS QR pass scanning for automatic pre-order cart retrieval with editable walk-in adjustments, establishing the 15-minute seating reservation holding policy with concierge overdue alerts, and perfecting Brew-to-Serve ingredient deductions upon drink fulfillment.
[113] (Heading 3) Phase 6: System Testing
[114] (Body Text Indented) Comprehensive unit, integration, and security testing was executed across local network environments. Tests verified the mathematical accuracy of 12% VAT calculations and discount deductions, confirmed atomic transaction integrity across database tables, validated ingredient stock decrements upon order serve, and verified error-free direct thermal receipt printing.
[115] (Heading 3) Phase 7: Deployment and Maintenance
[116] (Body Text Indented) The completed system was deployed on an institutional web and local area network server at ISAT U - Dumangas Campus. Operational manuals and training orientations were provided to counter cashiers and baristas. Formal software quality evaluation was conducted in accordance with the ISO/IEC 25010 international standard.

[142] (Body Text Indented) Figure 5a illustrates the Activity Diagram for the Customer role. The workflow begins at the solid black Start circle, proceeding directly into the public Customer Booking Interface without requiring user login or account registration. From the portal interface, execution forks into parallel activities: browsing available dining tables and study pod seating layouts, reserving a desired seat or table, selecting and customizing beverage pre-orders, and agreeing to the mandatory 15-minute seating reservation policy. Upon agreement and submission, the system generates a dynamic QR booking pass with scheduled table assignment details. The customer presents this QR pass to the staff concierge for onsite seating check-in and to the cashier for POS order retrieval, before terminating cleanly at the bullseye final node.

[154] (Heading 3) Class Diagram and Database Schema
[155] (Body Text Indented) Figure 6 presents the Class Diagram and database schema of the system, comprising eleven interconnected entity classes in MySQL organized into three operational subsystems:
[156] (Body Text Indented) 1. Catalog, Recipes, and Inventory Subsystem: categories (menu classifications), products (unique product_code QR tags, names, base prices, availability), inventory_items & recipes (raw ingredients, cup stocks, bill of materials quantity per unit), and product_addons & sizes (extra toppings, milk options, and cup size price tiers).
[157] (Body Text Indented) 2. Tables, Reservations, and Stock Ledger Subsystem: dining_tables (table and study pod codes, seating zones, power outlet availability, status), reservations (reservation numbers, unique qr_token hashes, time slots, check-in status), reservation_items (pre-ordered menu items and quantities), and inventory_ledger & txns (immutable audit trail of stock receipts, conversions, and Brew-to-Serve deductions).
[158] (Body Text Indented) 3. Users, POS Orders, and Payments Subsystem: users (account credentials, role assignments, student/faculty institutional affiliation), orders (transaction numbers, table references, cashier IDs, order types, subtotal, statutory discount amounts, 12% VAT, net totals, preparation status), order_items (line items, cup sizes, modifiers, unit prices), and payments (order foreign keys, cash tender, change computations, GCash transaction reference codes).
[159] (Body Text Indented) Cardinality relationships (1 to many, 1 to 1, and 0..1 to 1) are strictly enforced via foreign key constraints, with product foreign key references linking products directly to order line items.

The PHP server validates the reservation, persists the records into the database, and returns a dynamic QR booking pass. Finally (Messages 11-12), the customer presents the QR booking pass onsite to the staff concierge for seating verification and to the cashier for seamless POS counter order retrieval.

[192] (Heading 2) Data Gathering and Statistical Treatment
[193] (Body Text Indented) Evaluation data was gathered through direct demonstration and hands-on testing of the system by selected evaluators. Evaluators were purposively selected to represent both technical experts and domain end-users:
[194] (Body Text Indented) A total of ten (10) respondents were selected using purposive sampling to evaluate the system. Purposive sampling ensured that evaluators possessed relevant technical expertise and familiarity with campus auxiliary services. The evaluators were categorized into two distinct panels:
[195] (Body Text Indented) 1. IT Experts (5 respondents): Information Technology faculty members and software engineers from ISAT U - DC who rigorously evaluated system architectural design, database normalization, barcode auto-read responsiveness, security enforcement, and source code maintainability.
[196] (Body Text Indented) 2. Domain End-Users (5 respondents): ISAT U - DC auxiliary services staff, prospective coffee shop cashiers, student assistants, and faculty and student patrons who evaluated interface usability, study pod reservation clarity, order customization, and overall operational readiness.
[197] (Body Text Indented) The statistical treatment utilized for analyzing the evaluation responses was the weighted mean. The mean score for each quality characteristic was computed using the formula:
[198] (Normal) Mean = (Sum of All Item Ratings) / (Total Number of Respondents)
